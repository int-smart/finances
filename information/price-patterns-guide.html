Note: Price patterns are visual representations of market psychology - they show the ongoing battle between buyers (bulls) and sellers (bears). Think of them as fingerprints left by collective human emotions in the market.

What Are Price Patterns?

Definition:

Price patterns are recurring formations created by price movement over time that tend to predict future price direction with reasonable probability.

Why They Form:

  1. Human Psychology - Fear, greed, hope, and panic repeat
  2. Market Structure - Supply and demand create predictable behaviors
  3. Institutional Behavior - Large players leave recognizable footprints
  4. Self-Fulfilling Prophecy - Traders act on patterns, making them work
"History doesn't repeat, but it often rhymes" - Mark Twain

Classification of Price Patterns

1. By Direction Prediction:

Reversal Patterns 🔄

Indicate trend is about to change direction:

  • Head & Shoulders / Inverse Head & Shoulders
  • Double/Triple Tops and Bottoms
  • Rising/Falling Wedges
  • Rounding Tops and Bottoms

Continuation Patterns ➡️

Indicate trend will continue after brief pause:

  • Flags and Pennants
  • Triangles (most types)
  • Rectangles/Trading Ranges
  • Cup and Handle

2. By Time Duration:

Long-term Patterns (Weeks to Months) Examples: - Major Head & Shoulders - Large Triangle formations - Long-term trend channels - Monthly chart patterns
Short-term Patterns (Days to Weeks) Examples: - Daily flags and pennants - Small triangles - Brief consolidations - Intraday patterns
Intraday Patterns (Minutes to Hours) Examples: - Scalping patterns - Day trading setups - Hour-based formations - Minute chart patterns

Pattern Formation Psychology

The 4 Phases of Pattern Development:

Phase 1: Trend Establishment 📈

Psychology: "This is easy money!"
Volume: Increasing
Price Action: Strong directional move
Emotions: Optimism/Pessimism building

Phase 2: First Pause ⏸️

Psychology: "Maybe I should take profits"
Volume: Decreasing
Price Action: Sideways drift
Emotions: Uncertainty creeping in

Phase 3: Pattern Formation 🔄

Psychology: "Which way will it go?"
Volume: Usually declining
Price Action: Defined pattern emerges
Emotions: Indecision dominates

Phase 4: Breakout/Breakdown 💥

Psychology: "I need to act NOW!"
Volume: Sharp increase
Price Action: Decisive move
Emotions: FOMO/Panic kicks in

Pattern Quality Assessment

High-Quality Pattern Checklist:

✅ Structure Quality:

  • Clear, well-defined boundaries
  • Multiple touches of key levels
  • Proper proportions and symmetry
  • Fits textbook definition

✅ Volume Confirmation:

  • Decreasing volume during formation
  • Volume spike on breakout (2x average)
  • Volume pattern matches expectations
  • No unusual volume anomalies

✅ Context Analysis:

  • Fits with overall market trend
  • Appears at logical market location
  • Fundamental backdrop supports direction
  • No major events disrupting pattern

✅ Timing Factors:

  • Pattern developed over appropriate timeframe
  • Breakout occurs within expected time window
  • Market conditions favor pattern completion
  • Seasonal factors considered

Common Continuation Patterns These patterns usually occur during consolidation in a prevailing trend and hint that the trend is likely to resume. A. Flags and Pennants Flag: Rectangular channel sloping against the trend, usually following a sharp move. Example: In an uptrend, a flag slopes slightly downwards before breakout. Pennant: Small symmetrical triangle formed after a big move. Tends to resolve in the direction of the previous trend. B. Triangles Ascending Triangle: Flat top, rising bottom – bullish. Cup and Handle Resembles a teacup – the rounded bottom (cup) followed by a small pullback (handle). Bullish pattern, often signaling a breakout to higher levels. 🔄 3. Common Reversal Patterns These signal that the existing trend is likely to reverse. A. Head and Shoulders Top (bearish): Three peaks – the middle (head) is the highest. Bottom/Inverted (bullish): Three troughs – the middle one is the lowest. Break of the neckline confirms the reversal. B. Double Top and Double Bottom Double Top: Two peaks at roughly the same level – bearish reversal. Double Bottom: Two troughs at the same level – bullish reversal. Volume confirmation is important for reliability. C. Rounding Bottom A gradual shift from bearish to bullish sentiment. Typically signals a long-term reversal. 🔃 4. Bilateral/Neutral Patterns These show market indecision and can break in either direction. A. Symmetrical Triangle Can occur in both uptrends and downtrends. Indicates consolidation, and traders wait for breakout confirmation. B. Rectangle Price oscillates between horizontal support and resistance levels. Breakout direction decides the next move. 🔍 5. Tools and Techniques for Pattern Analysis A. Volume Analysis Volume should support breakouts. E.g., in a bullish breakout, volume should increase. B. Support and Resistance Patterns often form near key support/resistance zones. C. Timeframes Patterns can appear on any timeframe but longer timeframes are more reliable. D. False Breakouts Not all breakouts lead to sustained trends. Risk management (like stop-loss) is crucial. 📊 6. Entry and Exit Points: Entry is typically at breakout levels. Target is often set based on pattern height (measured move technique).
